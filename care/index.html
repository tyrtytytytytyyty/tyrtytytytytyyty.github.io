How it would work

Three steps.

1

Family texts a number

In their own words. "My grandma really likes this." They don't have to say who it's about. Their phone number is already on file with you as her contact, so the system knows.

2

It writes back

A short, friendly reply. Sometimes one small question, like "is that every week?" Then the note goes on that resident's card.

3

Caregivers see it first

The app knows the shift: who you're caring for, and when. You open it and see just your residents, with the personal notes on top.

Why text?

Because families won't download one more app.

Everyone already texts

No app to find. No password to forget. It works on any phone, even an old one.

It takes ten seconds

Families are busy. They think of these things in the car or at the store. A text fits in that moment. A portal doesn't.

No forms to fill in

They don't pick a resident from a list or type a room number. The number they text from tells the system who they're family to.

It sounds like them

Plain words, the way they'd tell you at the front desk. The system tidies it into a short note. Nobody has to learn a new way to say it.

Things you would know better than me.

Who is allowed to text in?

My first thought: only phone numbers you already have on file for that resident. Anyone else gets a kind reply asking them to call the front desk.

Does someone check a note first?

A nurse or a lead could approve each note before caregivers see it. Or it could show right away, marked "from family".

What stays out?

Anything medical. If a family texts about a medication or a symptom, it would not go on the card. It would point them to the nurse.

Privacy

Privacy and security would be designed together with your facility before any real resident information is used. This draft uses made-up people only.
